# Destination research

Goal: build a candidate list of places and activities for the destination and dates, broad enough to cover the group's likely interests, grounded in more than one source so you're not relying on a single opinion.

## Where to look

Search the open web, plus TripAdvisor, Google Reviews, Yelp, and the destination's local tourism board site(s). Don't stop at the first source — the point of checking multiple sources is that a place mentioned across several of them is a stronger candidate than one you found once. Use that overlap as a signal, not a hard filter: a single strong local-tourism-board recommendation can still be worth including even without cross-referencing on a review site.

## Tagging tourist vs. local favorites

As you gather candidates, notice which ones read as "everyone knows this" tourist staples (heavy TripAdvisor/mainstream presence) versus which ones come up more in local sources or as asides in reviews ("if you're a local, skip the crowds and go here instead"). Tag each candidate accordingly — the point of the distinction is to give the user both the safe bets and the differentiated picks, not to make local always better.

For short, dense destinations (a big city, a 2-4 day trip), it's easy to fill the whole list with the obvious tourist staples and stop there, since they're the easiest to find. Actively look for the local-favorite counterpart, not just whatever surfaces first — search phrasing like "hidden gem," "local favorite," or "skip the tourist spots" for the destination, and make sure at least some of the final list comes from that search rather than only the mainstream one. A list that's all tourist staples hasn't done the differentiated-picks half of this step's job, even if every individual item is a good one.

## Checking whether a place is still open

Before including anything, cross-check its operating status against whatever's the most recent source you can find (a recent review, an official site update, a tourism board listing). Sources on the open web go stale — a review from three years ago saying a place was "open, first came in 2019" isn't proof it's open today.

- If sources agree it's currently operating: include it normally.
- If sources disagree, or you can't find anything recent enough to be confident: include it if it's otherwise a strong fit, but flag the uncertainty rather than presenting it as confirmed-open. Don't guess and assert.
- If sources clearly agree it's permanently closed: leave it out entirely.

## When results are thin

For a niche or small destination, you may not find much. Broaden the search radius (nearby towns, day-trip distance) before concluding there's not much to offer, and say so in the output if coverage genuinely is limited — that's useful information, not a failure.
